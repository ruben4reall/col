How it works

It lives in the notch.
It stays out of your way.

At rest

Exactly the notch.

Nothing runs and nothing shows. The menu bar around it keeps every click.

A glance

Two small wings.

A song, a timer, a call about to start. Each wing is only as wide as what it shows and never covers a menu or an icon, the » button of macOS 27 included. When the menu bar is full, the activity slips beneath the camera.

Open

Everything, one gesture away.

Hover or click to open. Swipe down to open, up to close, sideways to change page.

Music and lyrics

Every song,
word for word.

Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer or YouTube in your browser: Col shows what plays, with its cover and its controls, and the lyrics beside it, each word lighting up as it is sung.

A real recording of Col. The song and its lyrics were written for this page.

New in Col 3

Lyrics on the Lock Screen.

While your Mac is locked and music plays, the words show large under the clock: the line being sung bright and filling as it goes, the ones around it dim, the cover blurred behind them so they read on any wallpaper. The controls stay at the foot, and the password field stays clear.

New in Col 3

And on your desktop.

The line being sung, large, with the next one below it, above your wallpaper and under every window. Clicks go through it, it never takes the focus, and it fades away a few seconds after you pause.

Sing along without opening anything.

Turn it on, and the line being sung sits beside the camera while you work. Too long for its wing, it glides across.

Private by design

Lyrics that stay yours.

Lyrics come from LRCLIB, an open database written by its users. Col sends it only the title, the artist, the album and the length of the song, once, and keeps the answer on your Mac. Your own .lrc files come first.

Word by word

Lyrics timed word by word fill as they are sung, in the island, on the Lock Screen and on the desktop.

Right on time

A little early or late? Move them half a second at a time. Not the right ones? Choose others, or hide them for that song.

Bars that follow the sound

If you like, the bars beside the camera move with the music itself. The sound is analysed on your Mac as it plays, never recorded or sent.

AirPods

It knows which ones you just put on.

AirPods, AirPods Pro or AirPods Max: Col reads the model your headphones report and shows each earbud, the case, or the single battery of the headphones.

AI

Your AI, right under the camera.

Col follows the coding agents you run and the models you host. It never signs in to any AI service: each agent reports through its own hooks, on your Mac.

Try it: answer Claude Code from the notch.

New in Col 3

Questions and plans, answered from the island.

When Claude Code asks you a question, its choices are buttons in the island. When it wants to leave plan mode, the plan itself shows, to scroll and select, with Approve and Keep Planning. Click a session, and its own terminal tab comes forward.

Every agent at a glance.

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor and GitHub Copilot, each with its own logo: what it works on, how long, and who is waiting for you.

Closed, still in the know.

The wings show the agent that needs you, its logo hopping like a Dock icon when it starts to wait, and how many are at work.

Ask your own models.

Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp or any OpenAI-compatible server, on this Mac or another on your network. See which model is loaded and when it is busy, then ask it a question from the island. Your question goes to your server, and nowhere else.

Found on your Mac, connected in a click.

Col recognises the AI apps you have, with their own icons, and opens them from the notch. Each coding agent connects with one button; Col keeps a backup of the settings it edits.

Prompter

Read your script.
Keep your eyes on them.

Your script comes out of the notch, right under the camera, and rolls at the pace of your voice. It waits when you stop, and nobody else ever sees it.

At the pace of your voice

Voice Pace rolls while you speak and waits when you stop. Voice Follow keeps your place word by word. Your voice never leaves your Mac.

Invisible to everyone else

The prompter stays out of screenshots, screen recordings and video calls. The island steps aside and comes back after the take.

In your hand

Drive it with ⌃⌥⌘P, a presentation clicker, your phone, Shortcuts or a link. Your scripts live in their own window.

Choose how it moves.

Voice Pace, Voice Follow, a steady roll or your own hand. Set the pace, the size of the text, and where the script shows: in the notch, floating, or across the screen.

The prompter was an app of its own, Souffleur. It is part of Col now: its scripts, settings and links carry over.

Make it yours

Your island.
Your way.

Choose what each page shows, the shape of the island, how big it opens, and the glass it is made of. It even takes your Mac’s accent colour.

Compose your pages.

Each page holds one widget across, or two side by side, and each place can stack several: the island shows the first one with something to show. Home puts the player beside its lyrics, and the clock beside your agenda or the weather while nothing plays.

New in Col 3

A pill on every screen.

No notch? On an external display or another Mac, the island is a small pill in the menu bar, clear of the top edge, the way the iPhone’s sits in its status bar. It stretches to show what happens and opens into a card. Prefer Col’s own notch, or a capsule below the menu bar? It is your choice.

Four kinds of glass.

From macOS 26, the open island stays black where it meets the notch and melts into glass below. Liquid bends the desktop like a thick pane; Transparent lets it show through; Tinted frosts it; Black keeps it solid.

Three sizes.

Compact, standard or large when open, at the speed you like: snappy, standard or relaxed. Closed, its wings reach as far as you let them: narrow, standard or wide, never over a menu or an icon.

Seen on your own screen.

Appearance shows your island on your wallpaper, drawn by Col itself with what it shows right now. Every change of size or glass also plays on the notch. And the island steps aside for the apps you choose, as it does in full screen.

Developers

A notch you can program.

Show any script’s progress beside the camera with one line. The same local API answers the command line, Shortcuts, links, extensions and, if you allow it, the devices of your network.

Terminal
~ % colctl push build --title Build --symbol hammer.fill --tint orange --progress 40%

New in Col 3

Widgets for everything.

A figure, a bar, a few lines and up to three buttons, in one documented format. Push them with colctl widget set, from an extension or from any device of your network, or let Col read a web address that answers JSON, only while the widget shows. A button calls its address only once you have said yes to it.

New in Col 3

Col Connect.

Pair your iPhone with a QR code, or Home Assistant, a Raspberry Pi or a build machine with a token: they push activities and widgets into the island over your network. Each device has its own token, kept in your Keychain. Connect is off until you turn it on, and Allow and Deny for your agents never leave your Mac.

Links

From any app, a browser or a script.

open "col://push?id=build&progress=0.4"

Shortcuts

Show an activity, end it, start a timer, open the island, drive the prompter: actions without a terminal.

Show in the Notch

Extensions

A folder with a script: whatever it prints shows in the island, on its schedule.

colctl extensions install <folder>

Widgets

A card of its own in the Widgets place, from any script.

colctl widget set temp --value 21.5 --unit °C

Col Connect

The same routes, from a device of your network, with its token.

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" http://your-mac.local:7580/v1/status

Open and close

The island itself, from a link, on the page you name.

open "col://open?page=live"

Privacy

It sits by your camera.
It keeps to itself.

No account, no tracking

No sign in and no analytics in the app. On its own, Col only checks for updates once a day and looks up the lyrics of the song that plays on LRCLIB, an open database; you can turn both off. The weather asks Open-Meteo for a position rounded to about a kilometre, only once you turn it on. Your questions go to the AI servers you add, and nowhere else.

Kept on your Mac

Clipboard history lives in memory, skips passwords and saves only the copies you pin. The camera runs only while the mirror is open. Your voice, and the music the bars follow, are analysed on your Mac and never kept.

Open to you only

Scripts and agents talk to Col through a file in your user folder that only your account can open. Col Connect, off until you turn it on, answers only the devices you pair on your local network, each with its own token.

Questions

Does my Mac need a notch?

No. On a MacBook with a notch, Col lives in it. On other Macs and on external displays it becomes a small pill in the menu bar, Col’s own notch or a capsule below the menu bar, as you choose in Settings, Appearance, Shape.

My MacBook Pro has Apple’s own Dynamic Island. Can I use Col?

Yes. In Settings, Appearance, Shape, choose Below the menu bar: Col floats just under the menu bar and leaves the notch to macOS.

Does it work on macOS 27?

Yes, Col 3 is made for it. macOS 27 draws every menu bar icon in one place and puts its own » button for hidden icons right beside the notch: Col watches that menu bar and measures it again the moment an icon comes or goes, so its wings never cover the » button, an icon or a menu. The permission it asks for there is called Device Control and Data Access.

Will it cover my menu bar icons?

Never. Col measures where the menus and the icons are on every app switch and whenever an icon comes or goes. The wings shrink to the free space, keep a single wing when an app’s menus reach the notch, and wait in the open island when there is no room at all.

What goes over the network?

The update check, once a day. LRCLIB, if lyrics are on, with the title, artist, album and length of the song that plays. Open-Meteo, if the weather is on, with a position rounded to about a kilometre. The AI servers you add yourself. The web addresses you give widgets, only while they show. And Col Connect, off until you turn it on, which answers only the devices you paired on your local network. Nothing else: no account, no analytics, no crash reports.

Which permissions does it ask for?

Only those the modules you keep need, each on the line that needs it: Accessibility (Device Control and Data Access on macOS 27) for the volume and brightness keys and to measure the menu bar, Calendars and Reminders for the agenda and your next call, Bluetooth for headphone battery, the microphone and speech recognition for the prompter’s voice modes, Local Network for the phone remote, Col Connect and the AI servers on another machine, Location only for the weather where you are, System Audio Recording only for bars that follow the sound, Automation of Terminal and iTerm2 the first time you click an agent’s session, the Downloads folder only if you turn on Downloads in progress, the camera only while the mirror is open.

Is it safe with my AI agents?

Col never signs in to any AI service. It hears the hooks Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor and GitHub Copilot in VS Code call on your Mac, a documented feature of each, and Allow in the notch is the same as typing yes in the terminal. Requests, questions and plans never reach the network, even with Col Connect on.

Where do my questions to my models go?

Only to the server you chose, on your Mac or on your network. Tokens stay in your Keychain. Col adds no service in between.

Windows says it protected my PC. What do I do?

Col for Windows is a preview and not signed yet, so SmartScreen may stop the installer the first time. Choose More info, then Run anyway. Every file is built from the repository by its workflow on GitHub’s own machines, and its logs show how.

Which Linux desktops does it work on?

KDE Plasma 6, COSMIC, Hyprland, Sway, niri and Wayfire draw the island above everything, through layer-shell. GNOME has no layer-shell: there Col runs through XWayland, under the top bar. It needs GTK 4.14 and libadwaita 1.5 or later: Ubuntu 24.04, Debian 13, Fedora 40, Arch and later.

Which languages does Col speak?

Col for Mac speaks English, French, German, Dutch, Italian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese, Korean, and Simplified and Traditional Chinese; the Windows and Linux previews, English and French. Every translation was made by AI, not by native speakers: if a word sounds wrong, please help fix it.

I use Islet. What happens?

Islet became Col with version 2.0: a col is the pass between two peaks, and the notch is one. The update renames the app, and your settings, permissions, pages and extensions stay as they were. islet:// links, the islet command and the hooks your agents already call keep working; in a terminal, the command is now colctl. Installed with Homebrew? brew upgrade --cask --greedy col gives you Col.app.

How much memory does it use?

About 27 MB at rest on a Mac, the music helper included, and almost no processor time: Col only wakes when something changes.

How do I remove it?

On a Mac, disconnect your AI agents in Settings, AI apps, then quit Col from its right-click menu and move it to the Trash. On Windows, Settings, Apps, Installed apps, Col, Uninstall. On Linux, remove the package, or run install.sh --uninstall.

Is Col made by Apple?

No. Col is an independent open source project and is not affiliated with Apple.
