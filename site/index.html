How it works

It lives in the notch.
It stays out of your way.

At rest

Exactly the notch.

Nothing runs and nothing shows. The menu bar around it keeps every click.

A glance

Two small wings.

A song, a timer, an agent at work. The wings grow beside the camera and never cover a menu or an icon.

Open

Everything, one gesture away.

Hover or click to open. Swipe down to open, up to close, sideways to change page.

Music and lyrics

Every song,
word for word.

Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer or YouTube in your browser: Col shows what plays, with its cover and its controls, and the lyrics beside it, line by line, the way Apple Music shows them.

A real recording of Col. The song and its lyrics were written for this page.

New in 2.0

Sing along without opening anything.

Turn it on, and the line being sung sits beside the camera while you work.

Private by design

Lyrics that stay yours.

Lyrics come from LRCLIB, an open database written by its users. Col sends it only the title, the artist, the album and the length of the song, once, and keeps the answer on your Mac. Your own .lrc files come first.

AirPods

It knows which ones you just put on.

AirPods, AirPods Pro or AirPods Max: Col reads the model your headphones report and shows each earbud, the case, or the single battery of the headphones.

AI

Your AI, right under the camera.

Col follows the coding agents you run and the models you host. It never signs in to any AI service: each agent reports through its own hooks, on your Mac.

Try it: answer Claude Code from the notch.

Every agent at a glance.

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor and GitHub Copilot, each with its own logo: what it works on, how long, and who is waiting for you.

Closed, still in the know.

The wings show the agent that needs you, bouncing like a Dock icon, and how many are at work.

Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp or any OpenAI-compatible server, on this Mac or another on your network. See which model is loaded and when it is busy, then ask it a question from the island. Your question goes to your server, and nowhere else.

Compose your pages.

Seen on your own screen.

Privacy

It sits by your camera.
It keeps to itself.

No account, no tracking

No sign in and no analytics in the app. On its own, Col only checks for updates once a day and looks up the lyrics of the song that plays on LRCLIB, an open database. You can turn both off.

Kept on your Mac

Clipboard history lives in memory, skips passwords and saves only the copies you pin. The camera runs only while the mirror is open. Your voice never leaves your Mac.

Open to you only

Scripts and agents talk to Col through a file in your user folder that only your account can open, never over the network. The prompter’s phone remote listens on your local network only while you turn it on.

Questions

Why the new name?

Islet became Col with version 2.0. A col is the pass between two peaks, and the notch is one: the cut between the two halves of the top of your screen. Three letters, said the same in English and in French.

I use Islet. What happens?

Nothing you set up changes. The update renames the app in Applications, and your settings, permissions, pages and extensions stay as they were; the AI and prompter pages join your pages. Lyrics, new in 2.0, stay off until you turn them on in Settings, Music and lyrics. islet:// links, the islet command and the hooks your agents already call keep working; in a terminal, the command is now colctl. Installed with Homebrew? The app keeps the name Islet so Homebrew can still upgrade it, and brew upgrade --cask --greedy col gives you Col.app.

Does my Mac need a notch?

No. On a MacBook with a notch, Col lives in it. On other Macs and on external displays it floats just under the menu bar, invisible until something happens.

Will it cover my menu bar icons?

Never. Col measures where the menus and the icons are on every app switch. The wings shrink to the free space, keep a single wing when an app’s menus reach the notch, and wait in the open island when there is no room at all.

Which permissions does it ask for?

Only those the modules you keep need, each on the line that needs it: Accessibility for the volume and brightness keys, Calendars and Reminders for the agenda, Bluetooth for headphone battery, the microphone and speech recognition for the prompter’s voice modes, Local Network for the prompter’s phone remote and the AI servers you add on another machine, the Downloads folder only if you turn on Downloads in progress, the camera only while the mirror is open.

Is it safe with my AI agents?

Col never signs in to any AI service. It hears the hooks Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor and GitHub Copilot in VS Code call on your Mac, a documented feature of each, and Allow in the notch is the same as typing yes in the terminal.

Where do my questions to my models go?

Only to the server you chose, on your Mac or on your network. Tokens stay in your Keychain. Col adds no service in between.

How much memory does it use?

15 MB at rest, the music helper included, and almost no processor time: Col only wakes when something changes.

How do I remove it?

Disconnect your AI agents in Settings, AI apps, then quit Col from its right-click menu and move it to the Trash.

Is Col made by Apple?

No. Col is an independent open source project and is not affiliated with Apple.
